Academic Plan of Study

All courses completed and currently in progress during the PSW program, with course descriptions.

Semester 1 — September 2025 to December 2025
Course Code Course Name & Description
PSHS 100
Anatomy & Physiology
Foundational study of the human body's systems, structures, and functions. This course introduces the anatomical terminology, major organ systems, and physiological processes essential for understanding client conditions and providing safe, informed care as a PSW.
PSHS 101
Foundations of Personal Support Worker
An introduction to the PSW role within the healthcare system. Topics include the scope of practice, ethical and legal responsibilities, client-centred care principles, the healthcare team, and foundational skills for supporting individuals across the lifespan in community, home, and institutional settings.
PSHS 102
Personal Support Communications
Develops effective verbal and written communication skills for the PSW setting. Emphasizes therapeutic communication, active listening, documentation, and professional reporting — including how to communicate sensitively with clients, families, and interdisciplinary team members.
PSHS 103
Developmental Changes & Alteration in Health
Examines human development across the lifespan and how illness, disability, and aging affect physical and psychosocial wellbeing. Students learn to recognize signs of health changes and understand how altered health states impact care needs and independence.
PSHS 104
Foundations of Mental Health
Introduces common mental health conditions encountered in PSW practice, including depression, anxiety, dementia, and schizophrenia. Focuses on person-centred approaches, reducing stigma, recognizing mental health crises, and supporting clients with compassion and appropriate boundaries.
Semester 2 — January 2026 to April 2026 (Currently Enrolled)
Course Code Course Name & Description
PSHS 122
Supporting Clients in Palliative Care
Focuses on providing compassionate, dignified care for clients and families during the end-of-life process. Topics include pain and symptom management principles, emotional and spiritual support, communication with grieving families, and the PSW's role within the palliative care team.
PSHS 123
Personal & Professional Growth
Supports students in developing professional identity, resilience, and self-care strategies essential for sustainable healthcare work. Explores reflective practice, ethical decision-making, workplace professionalism, and the development of the e-portfolio as a career-readiness tool.
PSHS 125
Clinical Practice
Hands-on supervised clinical placement in an acute care setting (Centenary Hospital, Scarborough Health Network). Students apply all foundational knowledge and skills in a real clinical environment, receiving formal midterm and final evaluations on competency, professionalism, and client care quality.
PSHS 126
Community Placement
Supervised placement in a community long-term care setting (Seven Oaks, City of Toronto). Students experience supporting residents in a residential care environment, developing skills in holistic client support, team collaboration, and community-based care delivery.

Global Citizenship

Group assignment completed in PSHS 104 — Foundations of Mental Health.

About the Assignment

The Global Citizenship assignment was a collaborative group project completed as part of PSHS 104 — Foundations of Mental Health. Our group explored how cultural background, identity, and global diversity intersect with mental health care in the Canadian healthcare context.

The assignment challenged us to examine how PSWs can provide culturally competent, stigma-free, and globally aware care to clients from all walks of life. We researched mental health perspectives across different cultures and how these perspectives affect a client's willingness to seek help, communicate distress, and engage with care providers.

Key themes we explored included: cultural humility in practice, the impact of immigration and displacement on mental health, systemic barriers faced by marginalized communities, and strategies for inclusive, person-centred mental health support as a PSW.

My contribution: I contributed research on the intersection of technology access and mental health in global populations, and helped design the presentation structure. This experience reinforced my belief that being a good PSW means understanding and honouring the whole person — their culture, history, and worldview.
